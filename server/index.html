Kashmiris as a Minority

Rosy Rose, 20/02/26 11:11 PM

The treatment of Kashmiris has been fraught with violence and oppression, reflecting a broader context of human rights violations in the region. Reports of abuse by security forces have surfaced, including instances of arbitrary detentions, torture, and extrajudicial killings. For example, the Kunan Poshpora incident, where soldiers allegedly raped and assaulted women during an army operation in 1991, remains emblematic of the traumatic history faced by local populations. This cycle of violence has created an atmosphere of fear, where civilians frequently report harassment and intimidation, undermining their basic rights and access to justice.

Kashmiris are not only struggling with direct acts of violence; they also face significant socio-economic challenges exacerbated by ongoing conflict. In recent years, the economic landscape has deteriorated, with many locals seeing their livelihoods affected by restrictions and military operations. For instance, reports indicate that drug abuse, particularly heroin addiction, has surged dramatically, with health facilities recording thousands of patients annually. The mental health impact is profound, as families grapple with the ongoing trauma of violence and economic despair, making it difficult for communities to envision a stable future.

The pervasive mistreatment felt by Kashmiris is further compounded by a sense of alienation from national narratives. Many feel excluded from discussions about their rights and identity, with the government's response often focused on militarization rather than addressing local grievances. Prominent human rights activists, like Khurram Parvez, have been detained under ambiguous laws like the Unlawful Activities Prevention Act (UAPA), accused of terrorism for merely advocating for Kashmiri rights. Such actions symbolize a broader strategy of silencing dissent, reinforcing the perception among Kashmiris that their voices are systematically marginalized in a landscape defined by conflict and oppression.

Did the Gov. Allow Pulwama and Pahalgam to Happen?

Rosy Rose, 2/12/25 9:10 PM

The Pulwama attack in February 2019 and the Pahalgam attack in June 2025 have drawn substantial criticism of the Indian government regarding its handling of security in Jammu and Kashmir. Following the Pulwama attack, where over 40 Indian paramilitary personnel lost their lives, many observers raised questions about the government's intelligence capabilities and preventive measures. The Pahalgam attack, occurring just before a crucial electoral period in 2025, reignited concerns over lapses in security and the adequacy of the government's response to evolving threats in the region.

The timing of the Pahalgam attack, coinciding with electoral campaigns, led to widespread speculation that political motivations may have influenced government actions. Critics suggested that the ruling party could use these tragic incidents to rally nationalistic sentiments and shift public focus away from pressing domestic issues, such as economic challenges and inflation. This strategic positioning raised eyebrows, with many arguing that the government was exploiting security concerns for electoral advantage rather than genuinely addressing the complex realities contributing to violence in Kashmir.

Moreover, the rhetoric surrounding these attacks further fueled dissatisfaction among the populace. Government officials often emphasized military responses and vows of retaliation, yet many critics pointed out that such measures rarely addressed the underlying socio-political issues in Jammu and Kashmir. This tendency to prioritize a show of strength over substantive dialogue only perpetuated feelings of alienation among Kashmiris. The lack of a coherent strategy for peace and stability left many citizens feeling marginalized and anxious about their future in a region marked by conflict and unrest.

Article 370

Rosy Rose, 31/11/25 10:45 PM

The abolition of Article 370, which previously provided special status to Jammu and Kashmir, has sparked a significant and multifaceted debate. As someone who has closely followed this issue, I can't overlook that many people view the move as a direct affront to the autonomy that the region enjoyed for decades. The article allowed Jammu and Kashmir to maintain its own constitution, laws, and decision-making powers in various areas, such as property rights and residency. By revoking these provisions, critics argue that the Indian government neglected the historical context of Jammu and Kashmir's accession to India in 1947, which was based on an understanding that the region would retain considerable self-governance.

Moreover, the manner in which the decision was executed has raised serious alarm among locals and observers alike. The heavy military presence during the revocation, combined with communication blackouts, resulted in a climate of fear and uncertainty. This abruptness prevented any genuine public discourse and deprived residents of their democratic rights. Many felt marginalized, believing that their opinions were not just disregarded but actively suppressed. This approach raised questions about the legitimacy of such a significant constitutional change, further aggravating sentiments of alienation and disenfranchisement already present in the region.

In addition to concerns about autonomy and governance, there is a palpable fear among residents regarding potential demographic shifts and cultural dilution. With the removal of special protections, many believe that an influx of outsiders could threaten the unique identity and heritage of Jammu and Kashmir. This fear is rooted in a history of communal tensions and past conflicts, making locals apprehensive about their future in a changing socio-political landscape. Ultimately, the opposition to the abolition of Article 370 stems from a deep concern for the stability, identity, and democratic rights of the people living in Jammu and Kashmir.

Shaheed Afzal Guru

Rosy Rose, 6/8/25 1:22 PM

Shaheed Afzal Guru is a prominent symbol of the struggle, sacrifice, and suffering of the Kashmiri people. He was born in 1969 in Sopore district, Jammu and Kashmir. Like an ordinary Kashmiri youth, his life unfolded under the shadow of state repression, injustice, and political uncertain... (truncated for brevity)

Afzal Guru was accused of involvement in the attack on the Indian Parliament on 13 December 2001. During the trial, serious questions were raised—not only by the Kashmiri people but also by international human rights organizations—regarding the evidence, confessions, and judicial proceedings. It was argued that Afzal Guru was not granted a fair trial and that the verdict was delivered on the basis of the “collective conscience” rather than concrete evidence.

On 9 February 2013, he was secretly executed in Tihar Jail in New Delhi. His family was not informed in time of the execution, nor were they given an opportunity for a final meeting, which in itself was a human tragedy. He was buried within the jail premises without handing over his body to the family, further deepening the wounds of the Kashmiri people.

The martyrdom of Afzal Guru is not merely the death of an individual but a question mark over an entire system of justice. After his execution, there was a strong reaction in Kashmir, and he began to be remembered as a person who fell victim to state injustice. Even today, many Kashmiris regard him as a symbol of their struggle for freedom.

History bears witness that voices suppressed by force often grow stronger with time. The sacrifice of Shaheed Afzal Guru is another link in that chain, which remains alive in the hearts of the Kashmiri people and will continue to convey a message of justice, rights, and freedom to future generations.

Imtiaz Ahmad Bhat
Vice Chairman, Jammu and Kashmir Awami Party
